Here's a frustrating pattern: you use AI to build a beautiful website, launch it, wait a few weeks... and it never shows up in Google. Not for your business name, not for anything.

The problem usually isn't the design. It's that AI tools are great at making a site *look* like a real website, but they don't automatically make it *readable* to Google the way a human visitor reads it. Those are two different skills, and most AI website builders only nail one of them.

## The gap, in plain English

Search engines don't "see" your website the way you do. They read the underlying structure — the title, the headings, the descriptions baked into the code — to figure out what your page is about and who should see it. If that structure is missing or generic, Google has almost nothing to go on, even if the page looks perfect to a person.

Here's what commonly goes missing when AI generates a site quickly:

## 1. Every page has the same generic title

Check your browser tab when you visit each page of your site. If every single page says something like "Home | My Business" or just your business name with no description, that's a problem. Each page needs its own specific title that says what that page is actually about.

**Fix:** Give each page a title like "Wedding Photography in Austin, TX | [Your Business]" instead of just "[Your Business]."

## 2. No meta description

The meta description is the couple of sentences that show up under your link in Google search results. AI tools frequently skip this entirely or leave a placeholder.

**Fix:** Ask your AI tool directly: "Write a 150-character meta description for this page that explains what it offers." Do this for every page, not just the homepage.

## 3. Images have no alt text

Alt text describes an image in words — it's how Google (and screen readers) understand what a picture shows. AI-generated sites often import images with blank or default alt text like "image1.jpg."

**Fix:** Ask your AI tool to add descriptive alt text to every image — "a golden retriever puppy sitting on a porch," not "IMG_2044."

## 4. Headings aren't structured properly

A page should have one clear main heading, then subheadings underneath it, in order — like a table of contents. AI tools sometimes generate visually styled text that *looks* like a heading but isn't actually coded as one, which means Google can't tell what's important on the page.

**Fix:** Ask directly: "Are the headings on this page using proper heading tags (H1, H2, H3), or just styled text?"

## 5. The site has no sitemap or robots file

These are small technical files that tell Google which pages exist and that it's allowed to crawl them. Some AI builders generate these automatically. Others don't — and if Google doesn't know your pages exist, it can't rank them.

**Fix:** Search "[your builder name] sitemap" to see if one is generated automatically, or ask your AI coding assistant to create a basic `sitemap.xml`.

## The takeaway

None of this requires becoming an SEO expert. It requires knowing that "AI built my website" and "AI made my website findable" are two separate jobs — and asking your tool to do the second one on purpose, not assuming it happened automatically.
