# Clearly AI Weekly Marketing Machine

## Purpose

This document captures the strategy, product direction, and one-week build plan for creating the first practical AI system under Clearly, AI.

The goal is to move from broad AI education into a more specific, outcome-driven offer:

> Clearly, AI helps everyday people stop merely chatting with AI and start using simple AI systems that save time, reduce stress, and help them do real work.

The first system to build is:

> **The Clearly AI Weekly Marketing Machine**  
> A simple system that helps small business owners plan, write, and organize one week of marketing content in under 30 minutes using AI.

---

# 1. Context

You shared an article claiming that the first solo millionaires of the AI era are being created by people who know how to set up AI to do real work, not just prompt it casually.

The honest read:

- The article has a real insight.
- It is also wrapped in hype.
- AI can dramatically increase one person's output.
- But output alone does not create wealth.
- The missing pieces are market, distribution, offer, trust, retention, and willingness to pay.

The practical lesson is not:

> Use AI and become rich.

The practical lesson is:

> Build valuable AI-powered systems for specific people with specific problems.

---

# 2. Strategic Takeaway

The article's useful idea is that the important skill is not just prompting.

The important skill is:

> **AI systems building**

That means designing repeatable workflows where AI helps produce real business outcomes.

Examples:

- Marketing systems
- Customer support systems
- Sales follow-up systems
- Content repurposing systems
- Admin cleanup systems
- Lesson planning systems
- Research systems
- Document explanation systems

For Clearly, AI, this creates a strong strategic shift.

Instead of positioning only as:

> Learn how to use AI.

Clearly, AI can become:

> Learn simple AI systems that help you get real work done.

That is more concrete, more valuable, and easier to monetize.

# 3. Main Pushback

The article says to forget prompting, coding, and building an audience.

That is wrong.

You cannot forget:

- Distribution
- Audience
- Offer design
- Customer pain
- Trust
- Sales
- Retention
- Differentiation

AI compresses labor. It does not eliminate the need for business fundamentals.

A solo founder can now produce more than before, but the market still decides whether that output matters.

The danger is confusing:

> More output

with:

> More value

The winners will not be the people who merely produce more AI content or more AI apps.

The winners will use AI to produce something specific, trusted, useful, differentiated, and distributed.

# 9. The Weekly Workflow

## Step 1: Capture Business Context

The user fills out a simple worksheet:

- What business do you run?
- Who do you help?
- What problem do you solve?
- What do customers usually ask you?
- What offer do you want to promote this week?
- Are there any events, deadlines, holidays, or seasonal things this week?
- What tone should your marketing use?
- What platforms do you post on?
- What is one thing you want people to do after seeing your content?

## Step 2: Choose Weekly Focus

The system helps pick one primary focus for the week:

- Educate customers
- Promote an offer
- Build trust
- Share behind-the-scenes
- Answer FAQs
- Show results/testimonials
- Drive bookings or purchases
- Grow email list

## Step 3: Generate Content Ideas

AI generates 10-15 ideas.

The user chooses the best 5.

## Step 4: Draft Weekly Content

AI drafts:

- 5 social posts
- 1 email
- 3 short video/story ideas
- 1 review request

## Step 5: Simplify And Humanize

AI revises the content to make it:

- Clear
- Plain English
- Human
- Non-cringey
- Specific
- Trustworthy
- Appropriate for the target audience

## Step 6: Organize The Week

AI creates a simple schedule:

- Monday: educational post
- Tuesday: behind-the-scenes or trust post
- Wednesday: offer post
- Thursday: FAQ post
- Friday: testimonial/review/story post

## Step 7: Save What Worked

At the end of the week, the user records:

- What got engagement?
- What got comments?
- What led to conversations?
- What felt easiest to post?
- What should be repeated or improved next week?

This creates a feedback loop.

# 11. Core Prompt Library

## Prompt 1: Business Context Builder

```text
You are helping me create simple, practical marketing content for my small business.

Ask me one question at a time until you understand:
- What my business does
- Who I help
- What problems my customers have
- What I sell
- What makes my business trustworthy
- What tone my marketing should use
- What platforms I post on
- What action I want people to take

Keep the questions plain-English and easy to answer. Do not use marketing jargon.
```

## Prompt 2: Weekly Marketing Focus

```text
Based on my business context below, help me choose one clear marketing focus for this week.

Business context:
[PASTE BUSINESS CONTEXT]

This week's notes:
[PASTE ANY EVENTS, OFFERS, DEADLINES, SEASONAL NOTES, OR BUSINESS PRIORITIES]

Give me 3 possible weekly focuses. For each one, explain:
- Why it makes sense
- What type of content I should post
- What action I should ask people to take

Then recommend the best one.
Use plain English.
```

## Prompt 3: Content Ideas

```text
I need simple content ideas for my small business.

Business context:
[PASTE BUSINESS CONTEXT]

Weekly focus:
[PASTE WEEKLY FOCUS]

Generate 15 content ideas for this week.

Make them a mix of:
- Helpful tips
- Common customer questions
- Behind-the-scenes posts
- Trust-building posts
- Offer/promotion posts
- Story-based posts
- Local/community posts if relevant

For each idea, include:
- A simple post title
- The main point
- Why my audience would care
- The best platform for it

Do not make the content hypey, fake, or overly salesy.
```

## Prompt 4: Weekly Post Drafts

```text
Turn these selected content ideas into 5 finished social posts for this week.

Business context:
[PASTE BUSINESS CONTEXT]

Selected ideas:
[PASTE 5 SELECTED IDEAS]

For each post, include:
- Suggested day to post
- Finished caption
- Simple call to action
- Optional image or video idea

Make the posts sound human, clear, and trustworthy. Avoid jargon. Avoid sounding like generic AI marketing copy.
```

## Prompt 5: Email Draft

```text
Create one simple email/newsletter draft based on this week's marketing focus.

Business context:
[PASTE BUSINESS CONTEXT]

Weekly focus:
[PASTE WEEKLY FOCUS]

Selected content ideas:
[PASTE SELECTED IDEAS]

The email should:
- Have a clear subject line
- Open with something relatable
- Teach or explain one useful thing
- Mention my offer naturally if appropriate
- End with a simple call to action

Keep it short, helpful, and plain-English.
```

## Prompt 6: Short Video / Story Ideas

```text
Create 3 simple short video or story ideas for this week.

Business context:
[PASTE BUSINESS CONTEXT]

Weekly focus:
[PASTE WEEKLY FOCUS]

For each idea, include:
- Hook/opening line
- What to show or say
- Approximate length
- Simple call to action

Make these easy for a busy small business owner to record without special equipment.
```

## Prompt 7: Review Request

```text
Write a short review request message I can send to a happy customer.

Business context:
[PASTE BUSINESS CONTEXT]

Make it:
- Friendly
- Not pushy
- Short
- Easy to send by text, email, or DM

Give me 3 versions:
1. Warm and personal
2. Professional
3. Very short
```

## Prompt 8: Improve My Content

```text
Improve the marketing content below.

Content:
[PASTE CONTENT]

Make it:
- Clearer
- More human
- Less generic
- More specific
- Easier to understand
- More likely to build trust

Do not make it hypey. Do not add fake claims. Keep the tone natural.

After improving it, briefly explain what you changed and why.
```

## Prompt 9: Weekly Schedule

```text
Create a simple weekly posting schedule from the content below.

Content:
[PASTE POSTS, EMAIL, VIDEO IDEAS]

Organize it by day.

For each day, include:
- What to post
- Platform
- Goal of the post
- Simple call to action

Make the schedule realistic for a busy small business owner.
```

## Prompt 10: Weekly Results Review

```text
Help me review how my marketing content performed this week.

Here is what I posted:
[PASTE POSTS]

Here are the results:
[PASTE LIKES, COMMENTS, REPLIES, CLICKS, CONVERSATIONS, SALES, OR OBSERVATIONS]

Tell me:
- What seemed to work
- What did not seem to work
- What I should repeat
- What I should improve
- What my focus should be next week

Use plain English and be honest.
```

# 14. What Not To Do Yet

Do not start with:

- A full SaaS app
- A complicated dashboard
- User accounts
- Stripe integration
- A huge course
- Too many audiences
- Too many workflows
- Perfect branding
- Complex automations
- A giant Claude/ChatGPT agent setup

The risk is building infrastructure before proving demand.

First prove:

- People understand the promise.
- People want the outcome.
- People can follow the workflow.
- AI produces useful enough outputs.
- People would use it again.
- People might pay for it.

# 15. Success Metrics For The Beta

The beta is successful if:

- 5-10 people agree to try it.
- At least 3 people complete it.
- At least 2 people say they would use it again.
- At least 1 person says they would pay for it.
- The workflow takes less than 45 minutes for most users.
- Users understand the instructions without you explaining everything live.

Great signs:

- Someone asks if you have more systems.
- Someone asks if you can set it up for them.
- Someone says, “This saved me time.”
- Someone posts content created through the system.
- Someone forwards it to another business owner.

Bad signs:

- People do not understand what it is.
- People do not finish it.
- Outputs are too generic.
- Users need too much hand-holding.
- The promise is too broad.
- People like it but would not pay.
