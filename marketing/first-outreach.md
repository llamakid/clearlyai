# First Outreach — July 2026

Drafts for the "talk to your existing users" push. Send the personal emails from
your own inbox (nate@learnaiclearly.com), one at a time, no BCC blasts — the whole
point is that they're personal. Edit freely; they should sound like you.

## 1. Personal email to each of the 17 free-tier users

Send individually. Use their first name if you have it from the signup email address.

> **Subject:** Quick question from the guy who built Clearly, AI
>
> Hi [name],
>
> You created an account at Clearly, AI a while back — I'm Nate, I built it, and
> I'm emailing everyone who signed up (there aren't that many of us yet, which is
> honestly why your answer matters).
>
> One question: **what were you hoping to figure out about AI when you signed up?**
>
> Reply with a sentence or two and I'll point you straight to the lesson or prompt
> that handles it — free, no catch. And if you signed up and then life got in the
> way, the free course is 10 minutes and it's still here:
> https://learnaiclearly.com/course/0
>
> Thanks for giving it a look either way.
>
> — Nate

**Why this works:** at 17 users, replies are worth more than conversions. Every
answer tells you what to build, what to write about, and how people describe their
own problem in their own words (which becomes your marketing copy).

## 3. LinkedIn posts (professional audience, 35–54)

specific, immediately usable thing + a soft pointer to the site.

### Post 1 — the "decode documents" hook

> Last week someone showed me a letter from their insurance company. Four

## 5. Library / community center workshop pitch

The 55–75 audience rarely converts online but shows up in person. Libraries
actively look for free programming. Email the adult-programming coordinator:

> **Subject:** Free "Intro to AI in Plain English" workshop for your patrons
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Nate Guy, a local [Fort Wayne-area] founder — I run Clearly, AI, an online
> school that teaches AI to people without any tech background.
>
> I'd love to offer a free 60-minute workshop at [library]: "10 Things You Can
> Do With AI Today." It's hands-on and jargon-free — attendees leave having
> actually used AI to explain a confusing document, plan a trip, and draft an
> email. It's built for adults who keep hearing about AI and want a friendly
> place to start.
>
> No sales pitch during the session; I mention my website once at the end for
> anyone who wants to keep going. I can bring handouts and adapt to whatever
> room/AV setup you have.
>
> Would a weekday afternoon this month or next work?
>
> — Nate Guy
> nate@learnaiclearly.com · learnaiclearly.com

One workshop = 15–30 warm, in-person signups from your hardest-to-reach audience.
Bring the starter-kit PDF as the handout with the URL on it.

## Two-week rhythm (repeat)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Mon | Post on LinkedIn (rotate the 3 posts above, then write new ones from user replies) |
| Tue | Send 4–5 personal emails to existing users |
| Wed | Reply to everyone; note recurring themes in a doc |
| Thu | One Facebook group post OR one library pitch email |
| Fri | 30 min: write next week's post from something a real user said |

The drip sequence handles everyone new automatically — this list is for the
humans you already have.
